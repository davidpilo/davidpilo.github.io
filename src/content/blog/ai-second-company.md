title: "We Are an AI-Second Company"
date: 2026-08-21
---

*We are an AI-second company—and we don’t lead with AI.*

Last week, while watching the World Cup final, I couldn’t help but notice Lenovo’s advertising around the pitch.

AI servers. AI computers. AI solutions. AI services. AI Lenovo.

I’m not exaggerating. That was the entire message.

It’s a perfect example of a trend that’s driving me crazy: companies leading with AI instead of leading with what they actually do.

And it’s not just Lenovo. Walk around a trade show, browse enterprise websites, or skim LinkedIn, and you’ll see that the industry has collectively decided that “AI” is now the product.

It isn’t.

If the most important thing you can say about your company is that it uses AI, what does your company actually stand for?

Lenovo isn’t an AI company. It’s a company that builds excellent enterprise infrastructure and some of the best laptops ever made. I owned—and loved—several ThinkPad T-series laptops back in the day.

AI is an ingredient. A capability. Infrastructure that, in many cases, is powered by someone else’s models.

Customers don’t wake up wanting AI.

They wake up wanting a better laptop. A more reliable server. A better CRM.

That’s why we barely mention AI in our own messaging.

Not because we don’t believe in it. Quite the opposite: we probably ship more AI capabilities than many of our competitors.

We just don’t think “contains AI” is a compelling value proposition—or a meaningful differentiator.

People buy products. AI can make your product better, just as it can make your competitors’ products better.

Stop worrying about leading with AI. Build a better product—and lead with that.
